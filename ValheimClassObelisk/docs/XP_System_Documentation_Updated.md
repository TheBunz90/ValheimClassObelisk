# XP System Documentation

**Project:** Valheim Weapon Classes  
**System:** Class XP / Creature XP Balance  
**Status:** v1.0 playtest design baseline  
**Last Updated:** September 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This document captures the current design decisions for the **Weapon Classes XP system**, including the reasoning behind those decisions. Its purpose is to preserve the context behind the numbers so future balance changes can be evaluated against the original goals rather than treating individual XP values in isolation.

The XP system is intended to support two related experiences:

1. A player's **first class** should level naturally alongside normal Valheim progression, reaching Level 50 on average around the **Mountain biome**.
2. Once the player reaches Level 50 and unlocks the ability to have **two classes active**, leveling additional classes should become progressively easier as the player reaches later biomes.

This is intentional. Weapon Classes contains **hidden perks**, so experimentation, swapping classes, and discovering the perks of several classes during one playthrough are important parts of the mod.

---

## 2. High-Level Progression Philosophy

### First Level 50

The desired target is for a normally progressing player who primarily uses one class to reach **Level 50 somewhere around the Mountain biome**.

Players who deliberately farm XP may reach Level 50 earlier, potentially during late Swamp or early Mountain. This is acceptable because they have intentionally invested additional time into class progression.

### What Level 50 Means

Level 50 should **not** represent the end of the XP system.

Reaching Level 50 in at least one class unlocks the ability to have **two classes active**, changing the player's progression loop from primarily developing one class into experimenting with and mastering additional classes.

Conceptually:

- **Before first Level 50:** Develop and discover a primary class.
- **After first Level 50:** Begin building a collection of mastered classes and discovering their hidden perks.

### Endgame XP Acceleration Is Intentional

The system does **not** need to preserve the same leveling speed throughout the entire game.

Later biomes should be substantially more rewarding than earlier biomes. By Ashlands and especially Deep North, leveling additional classes can become extremely fast.

Deep North being the best XP-farming location is considered **desirable**, not a balance problem. It is the final biome, so there is no later progression tier that needs protection from Deep North farming efficiency.

## 3. Creature XP Design Model

Base creature XP is **hand-balanced** rather than generated by a rigid formula.

A formula based primarily on HP was rejected because HP alone does not adequately describe the amount of effort, danger, or time involved in fighting a creature. Conversely, a system based primarily on danger would over-reward fragile but lethal enemies.

Each creature is instead evaluated using several factors.

| Factor | Scale | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| **Biome Progression** | Meadows → Deep North | Later content should generally provide more XP. |
| **Danger** | 0–10 | Measures how likely the creature is to kill an appropriately geared player. |
| **Durability / Effort** | 1–10 | Measures time, resources, effective HP, resistances, and general effort required to kill it. |
| **Mechanical Difficulty** | 1–5 | Accounts for poison, ranged attacks, flight, summons, AoE, mobility, resistances, terrain pressure, etc. |
| **Availability / Farmability** | 1–5 | Identifies creatures whose density or accessibility may produce unusual XP/hour. |
| **XP per Hour** | Diagnostic | Used as a sanity check between different creatures and farming strategies. |

### Danger Scale

| Danger | General Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **0** | Passive / no meaningful combat threat |
| **1** | Essentially harmless |
| **2–3** | Low threat |
| **4–5** | Moderate threat |
| **6–7** | Dangerous |
| **8** | Very dangerous |
| **9** | Extreme threat |
| **10** | Among the deadliest normal enemies at the relevant stage of progression |

**Important:** Danger is kept conceptually separate from XP. A creature's Danger rating should not be artificially inflated simply because we want it to award more XP.

## 4. Durability and XP-per-Hour Principle

One of the most important design decisions came from comparing **Wolves and Stone Golems**.

A system that rewarded creatures mostly according to biome and danger could make a Stone Golem irrational to fight for XP because a player might kill roughly ten Wolves in the time required to kill one Golem.

Therefore:

> **Enemies that require substantially more time and effort to kill must provide substantially more XP.**

The goal is not for every creature to have identical XP/hour. Some creatures can intentionally be better farming targets. The goal is to prevent extreme situations where a difficult, durable creature is clearly inferior to rapidly farming trivial enemies.

Examples from the current table:

- Wolf: **30 XP**
- Stone Golem: **250 XP**

Approximately 8.3 Wolves equal one Golem in XP.

Other useful comparisons:

- Draugr **25** vs. Abomination **150** = 6 Draugr
- Fuling **45** vs. Fuling Berserker **175** ≈ 3.9 Fulings
- Seeker **75** vs. Seeker Soldier **325** ≈ 4.3 Seekers
- Charred Warrior **120** vs. Morgen **500** ≈ 4.2 Warriors
- Elaking **150** vs. Barka **600** = 4 Elaking
- Elaking **150** vs. Gammeltroll **800** ≈ 5.3 Elaking

Raw HP is therefore a **strong but diminishing** influence on XP rather than a direct multiplier.

## 5. Farmability Philosophy

Farmability is primarily a **diagnostic**, not a universal XP penalty.

For early and middle biomes, unusually easy farming can justify reducing a creature's reward if it would otherwise encourage players to regress to older content instead of progressing through the game.

Examples include:

- Surtlings dying through environmental interactions.
- Very fragile enemies with high lethality, such as Deathsquitos.
- Dense groups of low-HP creatures such as Seeker Broods.

### Deep North Exception

Deep North is intentionally treated differently.

Because it is the final biome, high accessibility and strong XP/hour are acceptable. Players reaching Deep North should be able to use it as an **XP oasis** for leveling and exploring additional classes.

For this reason, Elaking XP was increased despite their potentially high dungeon density.

The same principle applies to repeatable or sustained Deep North encounters such as Fimbulvinter. If players intentionally maintain a dangerous endgame encounter to farm Krigen and Hexen, that is considered a legitimate and desirable high-end XP strategy.

## 6. Special XP Rules

### Passive Creatures

Passive wildlife provides **0 class XP**.

Examples include Deer and passive Deep North wildlife such as Seals.

This prevents trivial wildlife farming from becoming part of class progression.

### Unkillable / Nonstandard Entities

Entities that cannot be meaningfully killed through normal combat, such as Shadow, provide **0 XP**.

Bosses and named/special encounters are mapped separately from ordinary creatures. Bosses always award their full XP value on every kill; there is no first-kill bonus or repeat-kill penalty. Their summon/resource requirements provide natural friction against trivial farming.

Intrinsic boss-phase components, such as Kall's Aspects, award **0 independent XP** because their combat value is incorporated into the parent boss reward.

### Neutral NPCs

Neutral creatures such as Dvergr require special handling so the XP system does not encourage players to deliberately slaughter neutral NPCs simply for class progression.

Current design direction:

> **A neutral creature should provide 0 class XP when the player deliberately initiates hostility.**

If a neutral creature becomes hostile through legitimate world circumstances, its normal XP value may still be appropriate.

This rule is conceptually accepted but may require implementation-specific refinement.

### Player Participation

Environmental deaths should not automatically award full class XP.

The current direction is:

> **A player must meaningfully participate in damaging the creature before its death to receive class XP.**

The killing blow should **not** be required because that would create poor multiplayer behavior.

The exact threshold for "meaningful participation" remains an implementation decision.

## 10. Star-Level XP Multipliers

Starred creatures increase both kill time and lethality, so their XP premium should exceed simple HP compensation.

The current multipliers are:

| Creature Tier | XP Multiplier |
|---|---:|
| 0-Star | **1.00×** |
| ⭐ 1-Star | **2.25×** |
| ⭐⭐ 2-Star | **4.00×** |

### Rationale

A 1-star creature has approximately twice the base health and increased damage. A pure 2× reward would compensate only for additional durability, so **2.25×** adds a modest danger premium.

A 2-star creature has approximately three times the base health and substantially increased damage. **4× XP** compensates for both the additional kill time and the much greater consequence of mistakes.

Example:

- Wolf: **30 XP**
- ⭐ Wolf: **67.5 XP**, rounded according to implementation policy
- ⭐⭐ Wolf: **120 XP**

Thus one 2-star Wolf is worth approximately four normal Wolves.

### Higher-Than-Vanilla Stars

Support for 3-star+ creatures is **not required for the initial version**, but an extensible progression was discussed:

- ⭐⭐⭐ = **6.25×**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ = **9.00×**
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ = **12.25×**

This follows the sequence `1², 1.5², 2², 2.5², 3², 3.5²`.

This extension is provisional and should not be considered locked unless support for modded higher-level creatures becomes necessary.

## 11. Boss and Named / Special Encounter XP

Boss XP is intentionally substantial. Bosses are progression-defining encounters and their summon/resource requirements naturally limit repeated farming.

Boss XP is **consistent on every kill**. There is no first-kill bonus and no repeat-kill penalty.

### Bosses

|---|---|---:|
| Eikthyr | Meadows | **250** |

## 13. Progression Calibration

The design target is:

- **End Meadows / early Black Forest:** approximately Level 10.
- **Late Black Forest / early Swamp:** approximately Level 20.
- **Late Swamp / early Mountains:** approximately Level 30.
- **Mountains:** Level 40 and the approach to mastery.
- **Average first Level 50:** somewhere around Mountain progression.
- **Dedicated farming:** potentially earlier.
- **Progression rushing / frequent class swapping:** potentially later.
- **Ashlands / Deep North:** intentionally rapid leveling of additional classes.

A representative Meadows-through-Moder model placed a normal combat-active player close to the 25K range once ordinary star encounters are included. This is a modeling target rather than a guarantee; actual playtesting should determine whether the curve needs adjustment.

## 14. Dual-Class and Multi-Level XP Behavior

Reaching Level 50 in at least one class unlocks the ability to have **two classes active**.

The existing implementation already supports XP awards that cross multiple level boundaries. Large endgame awards can therefore advance a fresh class several levels at once without losing overflow XP.

A Level 50 class should not siphon XP that could progress another eligible active class.

One remaining design question is how XP behaves when **two non-maxed active classes are simultaneously eligible** for the same XP event:

- Give each eligible class the full XP amount, or
- Split the XP between eligible classes.

This decision remains open.

## 15. Remaining Implementation / Playtest Questions

1. Exact definition of **meaningful player participation** for kill credit.
2. Final implementation of the **neutral NPC / Dvergr hostility rule**.
3. XP behavior when **two non-maxed active classes are simultaneously eligible**.
4. Whether fractional star-multiplied XP is rounded, floored, or accumulated fractionally.
5. Whether 3-star+ multiplier support is needed.
6. Playtest-driven adjustments to individual creature, boss, special-encounter, or level values.

## 16. Balance Guardrails for Future Changes

- **Do not balance exclusively around raw HP.**
- **Do not use Danger as a disguised XP score.**
- **Do not punish extremely durable creatures with poor XP/hour.**
- **Do not allow trivial early/midgame enemies to become the obvious optimal endgame farm.**
- **Do allow later biomes to substantially outperform earlier biomes.**
- **Do allow Deep North to be the strongest XP-farming environment.**
- **Do reward starred enemies above their raw health increase because they also carry increased lethality.**
- **Do make bosses feel like major XP events while keeping their reward consistent on repeat kills.**
- **Do preserve rapid experimentation with additional classes after the first Level 50.**
- **Do not make Levels 40–50 so expensive that the first Level 50 is pushed far beyond Mountains during ordinary play.**
- **Use XP/hour comparisons as a sanity check rather than demanding perfect equality between creatures.**

The core question for any future balance change should be:

> **Does this reward make sense relative to the player's actual combat effort, danger, current point in Valheim progression, and the mod's goal of encouraging multi-class experimentation?**
